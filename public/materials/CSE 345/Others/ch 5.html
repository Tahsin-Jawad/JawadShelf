5.1 Introduction to Boolean Function Simplification

In Section 3.4 of digital logic design, we introduced how Boolean functions are implemented using logic circuit diagrams. When implementing a Sum of Products (SOP) expression, each product term requires an AND gate, and their outputs are combined using a multi-input OR gate. Conversely, when implementing a Product of Sums (POS) expression, each sum term requires an OR gate, and their outputs are combined using a multi-input AND gate.

সহজ বাংলায় ব্যাখা (Concept of Simplification):
যেকোনো লজিক সার্কিট তৈরি করতে কতগুলো গেট (Gate) এবং গেটের ইনপুট কানেকশন (Literal) লাগবে, তা নির্ভর করে বুলিয়ান ফাংশনটির রূপের ওপর।
  • SOP (Sum of Products) ফর্ম: প্রতিটি প্রোডাক্ট টার্মের জন্য একটি করে AND Gate লাগে এবং সবগুলোকে যুক্ত করার জন্য ১টি OR Gate লাগে।
  • POS (Product of Sums) ফর্ম: প্রতিটি সাম টার্মের জন্য একটি করে OR Gate লাগে এবং সবগুলোকে যুক্ত করার জন্য ১টি AND Gate লাগে।
  • ইনপুট ইনভার্টার (NOT Gate) কনভেনশন: আমরা ধরে নিই যে সব ইনপুট সংকেত (যেমন $x, y, z$) স্বাভাবিক রূপ ($x, y, z$) এবং পরিপূরক রূপ ($x', y', z'$) উভয় রূপেই সরাসরি পাওয়া যায়। তাই ইনপুটের NOT Gate গুলোকে সার্কিটের মূল খরচ (Cost) গণনায় ধরা হয় না।

5.1.1 Circuit Cost Metrics & Reliability

The total manufacturing cost, power consumption, and physical size of a digital logic circuit are primarily determined by two parameters:

  1. Number of Gates: Number of AND gates in SOP or OR gates in POS.
  2. Number of Gate Inputs (Literals): The total number of variable connections feeding into the gates.
বাস্তব জীবনের উদাহরণ ও সার্কিট নির্ভরযোগ্যতা (Circuit Reliability):

মনে করুন, একটি কম্পিউটার প্রসেসরে ১টি আউটপুট পাওয়ার জন্য ১০টি গেট ও ৫০টি তারের সংযোগ (Connections) ব্যবহার করা হয়েছে। অন্য একটি ডিজাইন দিয়ে একই কাজ মাত্র ৩টি গেট ও ৮টি সংযোগ দিয়ে করা সম্ভব।

কেন সংযোগ বেশি হলে সমস্যা? যত বেশি তারের সংযোগ (Input connections) থাকবে, সার্কিটের Reliability (নির্ভরযোগ্যতা) তত কমে যাবে। কারণ যেকোনো একটি পয়েন্টে ডিসকানেক্ট বা শোল্ডারিং নষ্ট হলে পুরো সার্কিট অকেজো হয়ে পড়বে (Malfunction)। তাই সার্কিটে টার্ম সংখ্যা (Gates) এবং লিটারেল সংখ্যা (Literals) সর্বনিম্ন করাই হলো Simplification or Minimization।

In this chapter, we explore four primary methods to simplify Boolean functions:

  • 1. Algebraic Simplification: Using Boolean postulates and theorems.
  • 2. Karnaugh-Map (K-Map) Method: A graphical visual grouping technique.
  • 3. Quine-McCluskey (Tabular) Method: An algorithmic method ideal for computer programming.
  • 4. Iterated Consensus Method: An iterative algebraic method based on consensus terms.

5.2 Algebraic Simplification of Boolean Functions

Algebraic simplification utilizes Boolean postulates and theorems (such as Distributive Law, DeMorgan's Theorems, Complementarity, and Idempotence). However, algebraic minimization is often a process of trial and error because there is no simple rule telling us which theorem to apply next or whether the final result is in its absolute minimal form.

5.2.1 Two-Step Strategy for Algebraic Simplification

Step 1: Convert the original Boolean expression into Sum of Products (SOP) form using DeMorgan's Theorems and Distributive Laws.
Step 2: Inspect the product terms for common factors and perform factoring. If necessary, duplicate certain product terms (using theorem $A + A = A$) to facilitate factoring, then apply $A + A' = 1$ and $A \cdot 1 = A$ to eliminate redundant terms.

5.2.2 Detailed Step-by-Step Textbook Examples

📌 Example 1: Simplify $F = ABC + AB'(A'C')'$

Solution Step-by-Step:

$$F = ABC + AB'(A'C')'$$
  1. Apply DeMorgan's Theorem $(A'C')' = (A')' + (C')' = A + C$:
    $$F = ABC + AB'(A + C)$$
  2. Apply Distributive Law to expand the second term:
    $$F = ABC + AB'A + AB'C = ABC + AB' + AB'C \quad (\text{since } A \cdot A = A)$$
  3. Factor out common terms $AC$ from $ABC$ and $AB'C$:
    $$F = AC(B + B') + AB'$$
  4. Apply Complement postulate $B + B' = 1$:
    $$F = AC(1) + AB' = \mathbf{AC + AB'}$$

Final Answer: $\mathbf{F = AC + AB'}$ (Reduced from 8 literals down to 4 literals!).

📌 Example 2: Simplify $F = ABC + ABC' + AB'C$

Solution Step-by-Step:

Strategic Trick: We can duplicate the term $ABC$ using the Idempotence theorem $A + A = A$, so that $ABC$ can pair separately with $ABC'$ and $AB'C$.

$$F = ABC + ABC' + ABC + AB'C$$
  1. Group the terms into two pairs and factor out common terms:
    $$F = AB(C + C') + AC(B + B')$$
  2. Apply $C + C' = 1$ and $B + B' = 1$:
    $$F = AB(1) + AC(1) = \mathbf{AB + AC}$$

Final Answer: $\mathbf{F = AB + AC}$ or $A(B+C)$.

📌 Example 3: Simplify $F = A'C(A'BD)' + A'BC'D' + AB'C$

Solution Step-by-Step:

  1. Apply DeMorgan's Theorem to $(A'BD)' \implies (A')' + B' + D' = A + B' + D'$:
    $$F = A'C(A + B' + D') + A'BC'D' + AB'C$$
  2. Distribute $A'C$ inside the brackets:
    $$F = A'CA + A'CB' + A'CD' + A'BC'D' + AB'C$$
  3. Since $A'A = 0$, the first term vanishes ($A'CA = 0$):
    $$F = A'B'C + A'CD' + A'BC'D' + AB'C$$
  4. Combine the 1st and 4th terms by factoring out $B'C$:
    $$F = B'C(A' + A) + A'CD' + A'BC'D'$$
  5. Since $A' + A = 1$:
    $$F = \mathbf{B'C + A'CD' + A'BC'D'}$$

Final Answer: $\mathbf{F = B'C + A'CD' + A'BC'D'}$

5.3 Prime Implicants

Before diving into systematic methods like Karnaugh Maps and Quine-McCluskey, we must understand the fundamental concepts of Implication, Implicants, Prime Implicants (PI), and Essential Prime Implicants (EPI).

5.3.1 Definition of Implication & Implicants

A Boolean function $F_1$ implies a Boolean function $F_2$ (written as $F_1 \subseteq F_2$) if $F_2 = 1$ whenever $F_1 = 1$. In this case, $F_1$ is said to be an implicant of $F_2$, or $F_2$ is said to cover $F_1$.

সহজ ভাষায় ব্যাখা (Implication & Implicant):
ধরি দুটি বুলিয়ান ফাংশন $F_1$ এবং $F_2$। যদি $F_1 = 1$ হলেই নিশ্চিতভাবে $F_2 = 1$ হয়, তবে বলা হয় $F_1$ ফাংশনটি $F_2$ কে Imply করে ($F_1 \subseteq F_2$)। এক্ষেত্রে $F_1$ হলো $F_2$ এর একটি Implicant।
সহজ কথায়: কোনো ফাংশনের ট্রুথ টেবিলে যেসব মিনটার্মে (Minterm) আউটপুট 1 আসে, তার যেকোনো একক মিনটার্ম বা মিনটার্মের দল হলো ওই ফাংশনের একটি Implicant।
📌 Textbook Example of Implication:

Consider three functions of 3 variables $(A, B, C)$ defined by their minterm sums:

  • $F_1(A,B,C) = \Sigma(0, 2, 5, 7)$
  • $F_2(A,B,C) = \Sigma(0, 5)$
  • $F_3(A,B,C) = \Sigma(0, 1, 3, 5, 7)$

Here, every minterm of $F_2$ ($0$ and $5$) is present in $F_1$ ($0, 2, 5, 7$) and also in $F_3$ ($0, 1, 3, 5, 7$).
Therefore: $F_2 \subseteq F_1$ and $F_2 \subseteq F_3$. $F_2$ is an implicant of both $F_1$ and $F_3$.

5.3.2 Prime Implicants (PI) vs Essential Prime Implicants (EPI)

1. Prime Implicant (PI): A product term obtained by combining the maximum possible number of adjacent minterms. If you remove any literal from a Prime Implicant, the resulting term will no longer be an implicant of the function.

2. Essential Prime Implicant (EPI): A Prime Implicant that covers at least one minterm that is NOT covered by any other Prime Implicant. Every Essential Prime Implicant MUST be included in the final minimized SOP expression!

Summary Hierarchy of Terms for Exams
Term Type Definition Inclusion in Final Solution
Implicant Any single minterm or group of minterms where $F=1$. Not necessarily minimal.
Prime Implicant (PI) A group of $2^k$ minterms that cannot be merged into any larger group. Candidate for the minimal expression.
Essential Prime Implicant (EPI) A PI that uniquely covers at least one minterm. MANDATORY in the final simplified SOP function!

5.4 Karnaugh-Map (K-Map) Method of Simplification

The Karnaugh Map (K-Map) is a graphical representation technique developed by Maurice Karnaugh in 1953. It organizes truth table minterms into a grid of adjacent cells such that adjacent cells differ by exactly one binary bit (Gray Code order).

5.4.1 Gray Code Adjacency Principle

In a K-Map, row and column labels are arranged in Gray Code sequence: $00, 01, 11, 10$. This guarantees that moving horizontally or vertically between adjacent squares changes only one variable state. According to the Boolean identity $x \cdot y + x \cdot y' = x(y + y') = x$, combining two adjacent squares eliminates the variable that changes state!

5.4.2 Structure of 2, 3, 4, and 5-Variable K-Maps

1. 3-Variable K-Map (8 Squares)

Variables $A, B, C$: Rows represent $A$ ($0, 1$), Columns represent $BC$ ($00, 01, 11, 10$).

A \ BC 00 01 11 10
0 m00 m11 m33 m22
1 m44 m55 m77 m66

2. 4-Variable K-Map (16 Squares)

Variables $A, B, C, D$: Rows represent $AB$ ($00, 01, 11, 10$), Columns represent $CD$ ($00, 01, 11, 10$).

AB \ CD 00 01 11 10
00 m00 m11 m33 m22
01 m44 m55 m77 m66
11 m1212 m1313 m1515 m1414
10 m88 m99 m1111 m1010
K-Map এর ঘর বিন্যাস মনে রাখার টেকনিক:
লক্ষ্য করুন, কলাম এবং সারির ক্রমানুসার হলো 00, 01, 11, 10 (গ্রে কোড)। তাই ৩য় কলামটি হলো $11$ এবং ৪র্থ কলামটি হলো $10$। একইভাবে ৩য় সারিটি হলো $11$ (12, 13, 15, 14) এবং ৪র্থ সারিটি হলো $10$ (8, 9, 11, 10)। পরীক্ষার খাতায় ঘর বসানোর সময় এই ৩য় ও ৪র্থ সারির অদলবদল খেয়াল রাখা অত্যন্ত জরুরি!

5.4.3 Rules for Grouping / Looping Minterms

  1. Group Size: Groups must contain $2^k$ cells ($1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32$). Never make groups of 3, 5, or 6 cells!
  2. Group Shape: Groups must be rectangular or square blocks.
  3. Wrap-Around Property: Top and bottom edges are adjacent; leftmost and rightmost columns are adjacent. The 4 corners ($m_0, m_2, m_8, m_{10}$) form a valid group of 4!
  4. Maximal Grouping Rule: Always make the largest possible group to eliminate the maximum number of variables:
    • Grouping 2 cells eliminates 1 variable.
    • Grouping 4 cells eliminates 2 variables.
    • Grouping 8 cells eliminates 3 variables.
Figure 5.2: 4-Variable K-Map grouping for $F = \Sigma(2, 3, 7, 10, 11, 15)$
চিত্রের ব্যাখা:
১. লাল রঙের গ্রুপটি কলাম $CD = 11$ এর ৪টি ১-কে নিয়ে গঠিত। এখান থেকে পাওয়া যায় $CD$।
২. সবুজ ড্যাশযুক্ত গ্রুপটি ১ম সারি ($AB=00$) এবং ৪র্থ সারির ($AB=10$) ডানপাশের ২টি করে ঘর ($m_2, m_3, m_{10}, m_{11}$) মিলিয়ে একটি ৪-এর গ্রুপ। এখান থেকে পাওয়া যায় $B'C$।
৩. সুতরাং ন্যূনতম আউটপুট: $\mathbf{F = CD + B'C}$।

5.4.4 Don't-Care Conditions ($d$ or $X$)

In many practical digital systems, certain input combinations never occur (e.g., BCD codes $1010$ through $1111$) or their output values do not affect the system operation. These are called Don't-Care Conditions and are denoted by $d$ or $X$ in the K-map.

Don't-Care Conditions ব্যবহারের নিয়ম:
  • ক্যালকুলেশনে বা লুপ তৈরি করার সময় Don't-Care ($X$) কে প্রয়োজন অনুযায়ী 1 অথবা 0 ধরা যায়।
  • যদি কোনো $X$ কে অন্তর্ভুক্ত করলে বড় গ্রুপ তৈরি করা যায় (যেমন ২-এর গ্রুপ ৪ হয়, বা ৪-এর গ্রুপ ৮ হয়), তবে $X$ কে 1 হিসেবে ধরে বড় লুপ বানাবো।
  • যদি $X$ অন্তর্ভুক্ত করলেও গ্রুপের আকার না বাড়ে, তবে $X$ কে অবহেলা করব (অর্থাৎ 0 ধরে ছেড়ে দেব)। সব $X$ কে কভার করার কোনো প্রয়োজন নেই!

5.4.5 Product-of-Sums (POS) Simplification via K-Map

To obtain a minimized Product-of-Sums (POS) expression using a K-Map:

  1. Group the 0s in the K-map instead of the 1s to obtain the simplified expression for $F'$.
  2. Apply DeMorgan's Theorem to $F'$ (take the complement $(F')'$) to obtain $F$ directly in POS form.

5.5 Quine-McCluskey (Tabular) Method of Simplification

When the number of variables in a Boolean function exceeds 4 or 5, Karnaugh mapping becomes difficult, visually confusing, and prone to human error. The Quine-McCluskey (QM) method (or Tabular method) provides a fully systematic, step-by-step algorithmic procedure that is ideal for implementation in software/computers.

5.5.1 The Two Phases of QM Algorithm

  1. Phase 1: Systematically combine minterms to determine ALL Prime Implicants (PI) of the function.
  2. Phase 2: Construct the Prime Implicant Table to select the minimum set of Prime Implicants that cover all essential minterms.

  3. মিন্তার্ম 5 কে কভার করার একমাত্র উপায় হলো BD। তাই BD একটি Essential Prime Implicant!
  4. মিন্তার্ম 12 বা 13 কভার করার জন্য বেছে নিই AB।
  5. এই ৩টি Prime Implicant ($B'D', BD, AB$) মিলে ফাংশনের সবকটি মিন্তার্মকে সম্পূর্ণভাবে কভার করে ফেলে!

Final Minimized Function: $F = B'D' + BD + AB$

5.6 Iterated Consensus Method of Simplification

The Iterated Consensus Method is an algebraic method that systematically generates all prime implicants of a Boolean function without needing to expand the function into full minterms.

5.6.1 Consensus Theorem & Consensus Terms

Given two product terms $T_1$ and $T_2$, if $T_1$ contains literal $x$ and $T_2$ contains literal $x'$, and no other variable appears complemented between them, the consensus term is formed by ANDing all remaining literals of $T_1$ and $T_2$.

$$\text{Consensus of } (Ax) \text{ and } (B x') = AB$$ $$\text{Consensus Theorem: } AB + A'C + BC = AB + A'C$$
📌 Consensus Term Calculation Examples:

1. Consensus of $ABD$ and $A'C \implies \mathbf{BCD}$ (since variable $A$ appears in normal and complemented form).

2. Consensus of $AB'C$ and $A'B \implies$ No Consensus Term (because both $A, A'$ and $B', B$ are complemented; consensus exists if and only if EXACTLY ONE variable is complemented!).

5.6.2 Iterated Consensus Algorithm Execution

Step 1: Start with the given SOP expression.
Step 2: Compute the consensus term for every possible pair of product terms and add the new consensus terms to the expression.
Step 3: Apply the Absorption Law ($P + PQ = P$) to eliminate redundant terms.
Step 4: Repeat Steps 2 and 3 iteratively until no new consensus terms can be generated. The remaining set of terms represents ALL Prime Implicants of the function!

5.7 Master Revision & Exam Cheat Sheet

Chapter 5 Key Summary Points for University Exams

✔ Objective of Simplification: Minimize logic gates and gate input connections (literals) to maximize circuit reliability and lower power/cost.

✔ Algebraic Minimization: Uses theorems like DeMorgan's, Distributive, Idempotence ($A+A=A$), and Complementarity ($A+A'=1$). Fast but relies on trial & error.

✔ Prime Implicant (PI): Largest combined group of $2^k$ adjacent minterms that cannot be merged further.

✔ Essential Prime Implicant (EPI): A PI covering at least one unique minterm not covered by any other PI. Must appear in the final solution!

✔ K-Map Method: Best visual tool for $\le 4$ or $5$ variables. Uses Gray Code layout ($00, 01, 11, 10$). Remember wrap-around edges and Don't-Cares ($X$).

✔ Quine-McCluskey (Tabular) Method: Systematic algorithmic approach ideal for computer programs and functions with $\ge 5$ variables.

✔ Iterated Consensus Method: Uses consensus theorem $Ax + Bx' = AB$ to generate prime implicants without full minterm expansion.
