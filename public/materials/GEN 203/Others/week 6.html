Module 1

Environmental Pollution: Basic Terminologies & Types

Core Definitions (মূল সংজ্ঞাসমূহ)

Environmental Pollution:

Undesirable changes in the natural environment that have a harmful effect on plants, animals, and humans. (প্রাকৃতিক পরিবেশের ক্ষতিকর পরিবর্তন যা জীবজগতের ক্ষতি সাধন করে)।

Pollutant (দূষক):

Any substance causing nuisance, harmful effects, or uneasiness to organisms. They can be naturally occurring substances or energies, but become contaminants when in excess of natural levels.

Persistence & Decomposition (স্থায়িত্ব ও পচন)

• Rapidly Decomposed Pollutants: Removed quickly by natural processes (e.g., vegetable waste).
• Persistent Pollutants: Take decades or centuries to decompose and are very difficult to remove once released. Examples: DDT, plastics, heavy metals, nuclear waste.

7 Major Types of Pollution (দূষণের ৭টি ধরন)

1. Air Pollution (বায়ু দূষণ)
2. Water Pollution (পানি দূষণ)
3. Land Pollution (মাটি দূষণ)
4. Noise Pollution (শব্দ দূষণ)
5. Thermal Pollution (তাপীয় দূষণ)
6. Light Pollution (আলোর দূষণ)
7. Visual Pollution (দৃশ্যমান দূষণ)

Noise Pollution: Sound propagation with harmful impact. Slide highlights viral awareness banners from Dhaka: "হর্ন হুদাই বাজায় ভুদাই" (#ToxicCities).

Thermal Pollution: Degradation of water quality by any process changing ambient water temperature (e.g., power plant cooling discharge).

Light (Photo) Pollution: Presence of anthropogenic artificial light in night environment.

Visual Pollution: Impairs one's ability to enjoy a vista or view (e.g., billboards, cluttered wires). Quote by Hundertwasser: "Visual pollution is more poisonous... because it kills the soul."

Atmosphere Structure & Air Pollution Categories

Atmosphere Composition & Layers

The atmosphere reaches over 560 km (348 miles) above Earth's surface. It moderates temperature, protects from radiation, and supports life.

Conceptual Question from Slide 15: "What if atmosphere becomes 100% Oxygen?" Earth would experience uncontrollable fires/combustion and toxic oxidative damage to cells!

Pollutant Classification

Primary Pollutants

Emitted directly into the atmosphere from sources.
Examples: CO, CO₂, SO₂, NO, NO₂, hydrocarbons, SPM.

Secondary Pollutants

Formed when primary pollutants react with air components or sunlight.
Examples: SO₃, H₂SO₄, HNO₃, O₃, PANs.

Indoor vs Outdoor Air Pollution

• Indoor: Tobacco smoke, biomass stoves, kerosene lamps, off-gassing from furniture, paints, mold.

• Outdoor: Vehicle emissions, industrial smoke stacks, brick kilns, crop burning.

• Wind Dispersion Rule: "Dilution is the solution to pollution" - High wind speeds spread out pollutants over larger areas lowering local concentration; low wind leads to localized stagnated pollution pockets.

Module 3

Major Air Pollutants & Air Quality Index (AQI)

Major Air Pollutants Summary

Particulate Matter (PM) Categories:

  • • PM10 (< 10 µm): Inhalable coarse particles (street dust, road construction). Causes respiratory ailments & pulmonary function decline.
  • • PM2.5 (< 2.5 µm): Fine particles (combustion, smoke, haze). Enters deep lungs; increases lung cancer risk.
  • • PM0.1 (< 0.1 µm): Ultrafine particles (diesel soot). Enters bloodstream; high risk of heart attacks.
Carbon Monoxide (CO): Colorless, odorless highly toxic gas from incomplete combustion.
Carbon Dioxide (CO₂): 93% tropospheric CO₂ from natural carbon cycle; 7% from human activity (fossil fuel combustion).
Nitrogen Oxides (NOx): NO forms in high-temp auto engines. NO₂ forms HNO₃ (nitric acid).
Sulfur Dioxide (SO₂): 1/3 natural, 2/3 human (coal burning, smelting). Forms H₂SO₄.

Bangladesh Air Pollution Facts (Slide 11 & 39)

• Bangladesh loses $14 Billion / year ($38.3 Million per day) due to air pollution.
• 96,000 Bangladeshi children under 5 died in 2018 due to PM2.5 pollution.
• Worldwide: 4.5 million premature deaths/year from air pollution; $8 Billion lost every day.

Module 4

Environmental Phenomena, Water Pollution & Solutions

Atmospheric Phenomena

Photochemical Smog

Formed when NOx + VOCs + Sunlight react, creating ground-level Ozone (O₃), PANs, and aldehydes. Creates a reddish-brown haze above cities, more common in sunny/dry summer climates.

Acid Deposition (Acid Rain)

SO₂ and NOx react in air to form H₂SO₄ (sulfuric acid) and HNO₃ (nitric acid). Causes soil acidification, leaches soil nutrients, damages leaves/bark, and releases toxic metal ions into lakes.

Temperature Inversion

Occurs when a warm layer of air lies above a cooler layer near ground. Traps pollutants underneath (common in mountain valleys or cities bounded by mountains and ocean).

Water Pollution & Action Solutions

Point Sources:

Discharged from specific, identifiable locations (e.g., pipe from factory, sewage treatment plant).

Nonpoint Sources:

Broad, diffuse areas (e.g., agricultural runoff, urban street runoff, acid rain recharge).

Common Exam Traps (True/False)

  • • TRAP: CO₂ is regulated as a toxic air pollutant under Clean Air Act.
    FALSE! Slide 30 explicitly notes it is not regulated as a pollutant under Clean Air Act.
  • • TRAP: Low winds dilute pollution better than high winds.
    FALSE! High winds spread and dilute pollutants over larger areas.
  • • TRAP: Ground-level Ozone is a primary pollutant directly emitted from exhausts.
    FALSE! Ozone is a secondary pollutant formed via sunlight reactions.

Primary vs Secondary Pollutant Matrix

Primary (Emitted Directly):

CO, CO₂, SO₂, NO, NO₂, Hydrocarbons (Methane CH₄, Benzene C₆H₆), Dust/Particulates (PM10, PM2.5), Chlorine gas (Cl₂), Lead (Pb).

Secondary (Formed in Atmosphere):

Sulfur trioxide (SO₃), Sulfuric acid (H₂SO₄), Nitric acid (HNO₃), Ozone (O₃), PANs (Peroxyacyl nitrates), Sulfate & Nitrate salts.
